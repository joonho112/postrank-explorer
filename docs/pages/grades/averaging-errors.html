Averaging error shares across reports

2.19 · after the postrank theory book, chapters 8 and 16

Pooled errors, conditional mean error shares and zero-imputed means when disclosure varies.

When the number of stated directions varies across reports, the average error share depends on how the reports enter the calculation. Dividing the expected wrong count by the expected stated count gives more weight to reports that state more pairs. Averaging the report-level fractions instead gives each report its probability weight, conditional on stating at least one direction. A third calculation assigns zero to a report that states none. We apply all three to the same counts and probabilities for a lottery over reports and for a simulation with repeated datasets.

Numerical illustrations

  • Three outcomes. Pooled pairs give 2/5, the mean among disclosing outcomes is 1/2, and zero-imputation gives 1/3. The chance of disclosure is 2/3 and mean disclosure is 5/9. Changes to wrong and stated counts affect these summaries differently because their denominators differ. The silent outcome has an undefined fraction.
  • More weight on errors. Giving the error-heavy outcome more probability changes the pooled ratio to 4/7, the disclosing mean to 2/3 and the zero-imputed mean to 1/2. We normalize the weights to give probabilities for the outcomes. The number of units in each outcome stays fixed.
  • A complete-report lottery. The four-unit example mixes entire reports. Pooled expected counts give 1/8, while the mean report fraction is 49/400. The lottery probability weights each complete report and its disclosure. Expected weighted errors are averaged within a report before the reporting lottery is applied.
  • All silent. Both ratio-based summaries read “NA: no disclosure.” The zero-imputed mean is 0 because the convention assigns zero to every silent outcome. The probability of any disclosure is reported separately.
  • Twenty datasets. A simulated league gives pooled error .0892 and a disclosing mean .0891. The running curves and individual counts describe the averages over the selected number of datasets. The true values stay fixed while each dataset produces fresh estimates and its own graded report.

A ratio estimated from a small sample need not be unbiased for a ratio of expectations. The hand-set outcomes provide an exact single-observation example; one trajectory of simulated datasets shows variability, rather than measuring that bias. Simple rational values use fractions in the finite examples; simulated shares use the site’s decimal format.

TipIn R 
A <- c(2, 0, 0); N <- c(2, 3, 0)
c(pooled=sum(A)/sum(N), disclosing=mean((A/N)[N>0]), zero_imputed=mean(ifelse(N>0,A/N,0)))

Standalone: averaging-errors, for example ?mode=replays&replays=5&cap=1.
